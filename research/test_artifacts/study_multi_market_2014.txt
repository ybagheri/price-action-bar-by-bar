====================================================
 FINDING - there is no edge, and now there are enough
          measurements to say so with some confidence
====================================================
242,473 events across 4 markets and 2 timeframes, about 50,000 resolved
outcomes in each walk-forward half. Expectancy is +0.02R in sample and
+0.02R out of sample, degradation -0.00R. Win rate 43-44 percent.

This is the same answer as the single-market single-timeframe run, on ten
times the data and seven market/timeframe combinations. Every combination
lands within a few hundredths of an R of zero, and the H1 sets are
slightly NEGATIVE (-0.01R to -0.04R) while the M5 sets are slightly
positive (+0.01R to +0.03R). Neither is distinguishable from zero once
costs are considered, and costs are not included.

The single clearest signal in the whole study is negative and structural:
the H1 setups average 31-36 bars to exit while the M5 setups average about
3. Whatever these levels are capturing, it is not a stable intraday
effect, because an edge that requires 35 bars to resolve is a different
claim than one that resolves in 3 and needs a different cost and risk
model to hold.

WHAT IS STILL MISSING, and it is not small:
  - Costs. Every figure is gross. Realistic spread, slippage and
    commission on these markets would exceed 0.02R several times over.
    The export does not carry them, so this study cannot say whether the
    net figure is small-positive or clearly negative. It is not a
    profitability result in either direction.
  - Timeframe breadth. M5 and H1 only. No H4 or D1, so no evidence about
    whether the rules work on a swing timeframe at all.
  - One year, 2014, and only from a single broker's demo feed.
  - USDJPY exists at H1 only, so its +0.06R is the one figure here with a
    small enough sample to be worth re-testing before believing.

WHAT THIS STUDY DOES ESTABLISH: the measurement pipeline handles a
multi-market, multi-timeframe export correctly, and it does so without
silently scoring a USDJPY event against EURUSD prices. Each event is
measured against its own market's bars, and the report states how many
events were excluded for having no bar history at all (here: zero).

The negative result is the useful one. It was not obvious in advance, it
was not going to be found by tuning, and the tooling that found it is now
reusable on any broker feed, any symbol list, and any timeframe.
